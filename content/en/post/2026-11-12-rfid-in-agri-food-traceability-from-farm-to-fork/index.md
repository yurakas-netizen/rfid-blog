The modern agri-food sector faces growing demands for food safety, supply chain transparency, and resource efficiency. RFID (Radio Frequency Identification) technology creates a digital bridge between physical objects—from live animals on a farm to packaged products on store shelves—and information systems, providing an unprecedented level of control and traceability based on the "farm to fork" principle. Implementation allows reducing product losses, automating accounting processes, and ensuring compliance with strict international food safety standards.

## Technology Foundation: Standards and Tag Types

The effectiveness of RFID systems in the agricultural sector is based on the use of specialized frequency ranges and international coding standards.

### Key Standards
*   **ISO 11784 / ISO 11785:** Global standards for low-frequency (LF, 134.2 kHz) identification of agricultural animals. They enable the creation of an electronic "passport" for each individual.
*   **ISO 22005:** Traceability standard in feed and food chains, defining requirements for data registration at every stage of product movement.
*   **GS1 GRAI (Global Returnable Asset Identifier):** Coding standard for returnable assets (crates, baskets, kegs), allowing tracking of their movement and optimizing return logistics.
*   **EPCIS (Electronic Product Code Information Services):** Data exchange protocol for supply chain events, ensuring real-time visibility of goods movement.

### Equipment and Operating Conditions
The food industry imposes extreme requirements on equipment: temperature fluctuations, high humidity, aggressive cleaning agents, and mechanical stress.
*   **Livestock Tags:** LF tags (ear tags or boluses), resistant to external influences and compatible with milking and feeding systems.
*   **Industrial UHF/HF Tags:** Special transponders with IP67/IP68 protection, withstanding temperatures from −40 °C (freezing) to +250 °C (thermal processing). Used for marking crates, pallets, metal hooks, and kegs.
*   **Tags with Temperature Sensors:** Built-in sensors for continuous monitoring of the cold chain for perishable products (meat, milk, fruits).

## Case Studies: Proven Implementations Globally

### Smart Livestock
On modern dairy farms, robotic milkers automatically identify cows via LF tags, determine the required amount of feed, and record milk yields. Feeding systems dispense rations according to individual needs, which, according to studies, increases productivity by 5–10%. The electronic passport records vaccination and disease history, allowing rapid isolation of sick animals and increasing the biosafety of the entire herd.

### Returnable Transport Items (RTI) Control
Major fruit and vegetable producers use RFID tags on crates and baskets. The system tracks the movement of each unit, optimizing its turnover and reducing losses by up to 40% compared to manual counting. Upon shipment to retail networks, the system instantly generates a label for the packaging based on tag data, containing the full product history and ensuring data accuracy up to 99.9%.

### Integration in Ukraine
In Ukraine, digitization processes are synchronized with the state system for animal identification and registration (Agency for Identification and Registration of Animals) and European regulations. This allows Ukrainian producers to seamlessly integrate into the single EU market, providing transparent data on product origin and quality.

## Financial Modeling and Implementation Stages

**Project Cost:** Depends on the scale of the enterprise (farm, processing plant, logistics center).
*   **CAPEX:** Tags (LF/UHF), readers (stationary on conveyors, mobile terminals), software for integration with ERP/WMS.
*   **OPEX:** Database updates, replacement of consumables, infrastructure support.

**Implementation Stages:**
1.  **Audit and Standard Selection:** Determining types of objects for tagging (animals, assets, pallets), selecting frequency range.
2.  **Pilot Implementation:** Testing on one section (workshop, farm) to evaluate reading accuracy in real conditions.
3.  **Scaling:** Equipping all production and logistics lines, integration with management systems.
4.  **Certification:** Confirming compliance with ISO 22005 standards and regulatory requirements.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What are the main RFID standards for livestock?**
The primary international standard is Low-Frequency (LF) RFID at 134.2 kHz (ISO 11784/11785). It ensures reliable reading at close range, which is critical for milking, weighing, and automatic feeding.

**How do RFID tags withstand food industry conditions?**
Special industrial-grade tags with IP67/IP68 protection are used. They are resistant to extreme temperatures (from freezing to thermal processing), moisture, and aggressive chemicals. For metal objects (hooks, kegs), tags with dielectric substrates are applied.

**How accurate is "farm to fork" traceability?**
With proper system configuration and use of GS1/EPCIS standards, data accuracy reaches 99.9%. The system records every event: animal birth, vaccination, slaughter, processing, transportation, and sale, creating an immutable digital history of the product.

**How does RFID help in cold chain control?**
Tags with built-in temperature sensors continuously record thermal regimes during transportation and storage. In case of deviation from the norm, the system generates an instant alert, allowing prevention of batch spoilage and exclusion of substandard products from reaching the consumer.
